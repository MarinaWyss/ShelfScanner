## Suggested change

Two, one large and one small. Neither is approved by being written here.

**1. Make a stage primary that is not answering visible, and put credit on the
Google project.**

*What is wrong.* The reading primary has been failing on Google's credit quota
for most of this window, and nothing says so. Failover absorbs it: every scan
completes, on Sonnet 5. Worse, a primary that fails writes no row of its own —
the row records the model that answered, with `failover_from` and
`failover_error` — so the per-model report and `research.check` cannot see the
outage at all. `research.check` passed while I wrote this (reading recall
1.0000, 5 rows, 0 errors) by scoring the newest Gemini rows *without* an error,
which may be days old. The gate can't tell a healthy primary from an absent one.

*How the rows show it.* 14 app failovers and 480 test-set failovers, one kind,
one text (`RESOURCE_EXHAUSTED … prepayment credits are depleted`). The examples
run 2026-09-08 to 2026-09-11, and 480 is about seven and a half nightly runs
over 64 photos, which puts the start nearer 2026-09-04. Reading costs $0.0109 a
photo on the Sonnet rows the report shows, against the $0.008 the primary was
chosen for, and the nightly's numbers over that stretch describe the fallback,
not the model named in config.

*What to change.* Nothing in the pipeline; the router behaved as specified.
(a) Operational and Marina's: top up the Google project's prepaid credit. (b) A
primary-health line on the dashboard, next to the price check and in the same
spirit (002 D5): per stage, the configured primary, when it last answered, and
the share of the window's calls that failed over — in the accent colour when
every call in the last 24 hours failed over. (c) `research.check` fails, naming
the stage, when the primary answered no row in the run it is scoring, instead of
silently grading stale rows. No alerting: scoping section 7 says none, and this
is a line on a page plus a gate. Specs affected: `docs/specs/monitoring.md` (the
metrics list and the page) and `docs/specs/model-router.md` (the check
paragraph).

*How we would know it worked.* The next review has no `http 429` reading group
in either population; the dashboard shows a Gemini row from within the day;
reading cost per scan on app rows returns to about $0.008; and a seeded-row test
where the primary is stale makes `research.check` fail.

**2. Stop the review window overlapping the previous one.**

*What is wrong.* Rows judged in one review are counted and judged again in the
next. This week that is six named rows, a mark, and 60 of the test set's "scans
started" — enough to make the counts read as more activity than there was.

*How the rows show it.* Reading rows 188 and 189, choosing rows 50 and 51,
test-set reading row 94 and the *Stranger in a Strange Land* mark appear
verbatim in both `2026-09-03.md` and this file.

*What to change.* `research.review`'s `last_review_date` falls back to midnight
of the file's date when the file has no "drafted at" line; every generated file
has one, but the first was written by hand. Either backfill that line into
`2026-09-03.md` or make the fallback the end of the file's day. One line, plus a
test in `tests/test_review.py` with a previous file that has no timestamp. Spec:
`docs/specs/monitoring.md`, "The weekly review".

*How we would know it worked.* No row id appears in two consecutive review
files.

*Also worth a decision, not yet a proposal:* on a shelf with four verified
titles, one extra pick threw away the whole reply and the reader saw the
choosing stage fail (rows 174, 175). Whether a wrong count should discard a
reply whose on-list picks are usable is a change to R2 and
`docs/specs/recommendation.md`, and it is Marina's call, not a review's. Two
rows on one day is below the pattern bar; if the kind returns next week, that is
the proposal to write.
