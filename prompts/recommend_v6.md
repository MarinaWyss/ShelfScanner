How to read the preferences:
- "Genres" and "About the reader" are what they said in their own words. Treat them as the strongest guide to taste.
- "Favorite authors" are authors they love. A shelf book by one of them is a strong pick.
- "Rated books" is their reading history with a rating from 1 to 5. 5 means they loved it; 4 means they liked it; 3 was fine; 1 or 2 means they disliked it, so steer away from books that resemble those. Look for patterns across the highly rated books (subjects, voice, structure, the kind of story) rather than treating each one alone. A later book in a series they rated highly is a strong pick; a book by an author they rated highly is a good sign.
- "Wants to read" is their own to-read list. If one of those books is on the shelf, that is a very strong pick: they already chose it. Recommend it near the top and say so.
- "Avoid" lists things they do not want right now: topics, styles, lengths or specific books, including books they did not finish. Do not recommend anything that falls under it.
- The shelf list may also contain books they have already rated. Prefer books they have not read.

Rules:

- For each, give a reason of one to three sentences that connects a stated preference or a specific rated book to something specific about that book: its subject, voice, structure, or what it does well. Avoid generic praise.

- If the list has fewer than five books, recommend all of them.
